LOB go-to-market, pricing, and risk

Lumber One Board is a vetted B2B marketplace for forest-products freight: post → book/bid → dispatch → pickup/delivery check-in → mutual close → completion docs. This brief ranks monetization choices against cold-start reality, not against DAT feature parity.

Three assumptions that will kill this if wrong

1. Suppliers will not keep posting into an empty carrier pool. Carrier density on a few PNW / mill lanes must come before any paid plan that taxes carriers.

2. Charging like a general load board (DAT/Truckstop seat fees) makes LOB a worse, thinner DAT. The wedge is niche trust plus ops lifecycle, not search volume.

3. Taking a cut of freight money without broker authority, payment rails, and claims process turns a software product into a regulated brokerage. Do not stumble into that.

Go-to-market strategy

Win a dense vertical beachhead, then expand. Do not open a national empty board.

Who we sell to first

Mid-size mills & wholesalers Primary
Enough volume to need a board, not so large that Princeton TMX / private carrier portals already solved tendering. They feel Excel + phone pain.
Asset flatbed fleets Secondary
Carriers already hauling lumber who hate ghost loads and double brokers. Sell trust + clean dispatch docs, not another $149 rate tool.
Mega mills & owner-ops only Defer
Mega mills want ERP/EDI tender engines. Solo O/Os churn unless lane density is already high. Both are phase 3+.

Pricing strategies (ranked)

Ranked for a pre-liquidity niche marketplace with no billing product and draft legal. Dollar bands are directional planning figures, not published offers.

#1 recommended — Supplier SaaS + free carriers (density phase) Ship now path

Charge the scarce, value-capturing side: mills/wholesalers pay for posting seats, bid management, shipment lifecycle, credit exchange. Carriers free until corridor density is proven.

PlanIndicative priceIncludes
Supplier Starter$299–$499 / company / moPost + book workflow, limited seats, credit file, basic shipments
Supplier Ops$799–$1,200 / company / moMulti-user, exclusions/tiers, capacity inbox, priority support
Carrier$0 during Phase 0–1Browse, book/bid, capacity, dispatch, check-in

Why #1: Matches who feels pain, avoids DAT seat competition, does not require freight-fund handling. Failure mode: suppliers refuse to pay before coverage exists — so keep free pilot seats until Phase 0 metrics hit.

#2 — Hybrid: free pilot → supplier SaaS + per-completed-load fee

After mutual close, bill shipper a flat success fee ($35–$75/load) or small capped % of linehaul (1–1.5%, cap ~$150). SaaS covers platform; fee aligns with covered freight.

Stronger unit economics if volume grows. Needs invoicing, dispute rules, and counsel on whether the fee is a software transaction fee (preferred) vs brokerage compensation. Do not collect carrier pay.

#3 — Dual subscription (DAT-like)

Suppliers ~$300–$800/mo; carriers ~$49–$129/mo once density exists. Familiar to the industry.

Premature carrier fees shrink supply of trucks when LOB has less freight than DAT. Only introduce after carriers already get weekly booked miles from LOB.

#4 later — Insights / rate-data add-on

Paid monthly analytics (STATUS already flags this as future). DAT proves data monetizes, but LOB’s customer Insights are deliberately hidden pending privacy review. Internal lane intel stays admin-only.

Good year-2 ARPU lever. Bad launch wedge. Selling thin-sample rates damages trust permanently.

#5 avoid — Freight commission / LOB-as-broker

Taking 5–15% of linehaul or holding shipper/carrier funds. Forces broker authority, bonding, claims, double-broker exposure, and redefines the company.

Only revisit if LOB deliberately becomes a licensed brokerage with ops headcount. That is a different company.

90-day operating plan

Weeks 1–2: counsel kickoff; lock beachhead list; freeze any public Insights claims.

Weeks 3–8: run Phase 0 free for both sides; instrument cover time, completion rate, no-shows; founder sales only.

Weeks 9–12: if Phase 0 metrics hit, pilot Supplier Starter pricing with 2–3 paying mills; carriers stay free; ship billing + minimal alerts.

Kill criteria: if loads do not complete with mutual close, do not monetize. Fix trust and density first.

CEO verdict

LOB should not price like DAT. Sell a vetted forest-products ops board to mid-size suppliers, give carriers free access until lanes are dense, and refuse brokerage economics until counsel and liquidity say otherwise.

Top pricing choice: Supplier SaaS + free carriers, with free pilots until Phase 0 completion metrics are real. Hybrid success fees are the scale-up lever. Dual subscriptions and Insights are later. Freight commissions are a strategic fork, not a tweak.
